Born in a thunderstorm, destined to harness lightning — Nikola Tesla was a Serbian-American inventor, physicist, and futurist whose contributions to the design of the modern alternating current (AC) electrical system changed the course of human civilization forever.

Born 10 July 1856, Smiljan, Croatia
Died 7 January 1943, New York City, USA
Known For AC Power, Tesla Coil, Radio, Rotating Magnetic Field

The Story of a Genius

🌩️

Early Life & Education

Nikola Tesla was born on July 10, 1856, during a fierce lightning storm in the village of Smiljan, which was then part of the Austrian Empire (modern-day Croatia). His father, Milutin Tesla, was a Serbian Orthodox priest, and his mother, Đuka Mandić, was an inventive homemaker who crafted small household tools. Even as a child, Tesla showed an extraordinary ability to visualize complex machinery entirely in his mind — he could design and test inventions mentally before ever putting pen to paper. He studied engineering at the Technical University of Graz and later attended the University of Prague, though he never formally graduated. His professors recognized a mind unlike any other, one that would soon reshape the world.

⚡

The War of Currents

In 1884, Tesla arrived in America with little more than a letter of recommendation addressed to Thomas Edison. He briefly worked for Edison, but the two clashed fundamentally — Edison championed direct current (DC), while Tesla believed alternating current (AC) was the future of electrical power. Their disagreement sparked the legendary "War of Currents," one of the fiercest technological rivalries in history. Tesla partnered with industrialist George Westinghouse, and together they demonstrated the superiority of AC power by illuminating the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago and harnessing the power of Niagara Falls. It was AC, not DC, that became the global standard — a vindication of Tesla's vision that powers every home on Earth today.

📡

Inventions & Breakthroughs

Tesla's inventive output was staggering. He developed the Tesla coil, a resonant transformer circuit still used in radio technology today. He built one of the first hydroelectric power plants at Niagara Falls. He pioneered wireless communication — filing key radio patents before Marconi — and demonstrated a radio-controlled boat in 1898, essentially inventing remote control technology. He experimented with X-rays, contributed to the development of radar, and envisioned a world connected by wireless energy transmission. His Wardenclyffe Tower project on Long Island was meant to broadcast free wireless energy across the globe, but it was ultimately dismantled when funding collapsed. Many of his ideas were so far ahead of their time that the world simply wasn't ready for them.

🕊️

Later Years & Legacy

Despite his genius, Tesla spent his later years in relative poverty, living alone in a series of New York hotel rooms. He never married, devoting every ounce of his energy to his work. He fed pigeons in Bryant Park and continued to announce new inventions to the press, though many remained unrealized. Tesla died on January 7, 1943, at the age of 86 in Room 3327 of the New Yorker Hotel. After his death, the FBI seized his papers, fueling decades of conspiracy theories about suppressed inventions. Today, Tesla is recognized as one of the most brilliant minds in human history. The SI unit of magnetic flux density is named the "tesla" in his honor, and Elon Musk named his electric car company after him — a fitting tribute to the man who electrified the world.

While walking in Budapest, Tesla visualized the rotating magnetic field — the key to AC power.

Emigrated to the United States with four cents and a letter of introduction to Thomas Edison.

1888

Licensed his AC patents to George Westinghouse, igniting the War of Currents against Edison.

Developed the resonant transformer circuit, fundamental to radio technology and wireless transmission.

Lit the World's Fair

AC power illuminated the Chicago World's Columbian Exposition, proving its superiority over DC.

1895

Niagara Falls Power Plant

Designed the first major hydroelectric power plant at Niagara Falls using his AC system.

1898

Radio-Controlled Boat

Demonstrated remote control technology at Madison Square Garden — decades ahead of its time.

1901

Wardenclyffe Tower

Began building his ambitious wireless energy transmission tower on Long Island, New York.

1943

Passed Away in New York

Died alone at age 86 in the New Yorker Hotel. His legacy continues to inspire millions worldwide.
